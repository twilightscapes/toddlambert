Open Floor · working concept · draft 0.1

Every voter holds the floor.

Congress takes about 760,000 people, squeezes them into one representative, and lets that person's beliefs, donors and mood that day decide. Open Floor removes the bottleneck. You file directly. An open, auditable AI keeps the record. Every voice weighs exactly one.

§1 · The bottleneck

One person. One mood. 760,000 votes.

A House member speaks for about 760,000 people. Those people disagree with each other on almost everything. The member can only cast one vote. So every one of their beliefs, grudges, donors and bad mornings gets multiplied by 760,000.

That is the design. It is not a bug you can fix with a better candidate. A different person in the seat is still one person in the seat.

Emotion is not the bug. Concentration is.

Voters are emotional too. The difference is scale. On the Floor, 150 million feelings average into a signal. In Congress, one feeling counts 760,000 times.

§2 · The rewrite

Take the person out of the middle.

Representation was a workaround for distance. In 1789 a farmer could not ride to Philadelphia every week, so he sent someone. The workaround became the system, and the system became the thing to capture. Lobbying is cheap because the target list is only 535 names long.

Now · how a law happens

  1. You vote once every two years.For a bundle of positions you mostly did not pick, attached to a person you have never met.
  2. Your representative decides.Your 760,000-way disagreement collapses into one person, filtered through:
    party whipdonorsre-election mathpersonal beliefmood
  3. Staff and lobbyists write the bill.A thousand pages. Nobody who votes on it reads it.
  4. Law.Or, more often, a shutdown, a stalemate, a continuing resolution.

Open Floor · how a law happens

  1. You file.Any day, any topic, from your phone. A complaint, a proposal, a vote. It goes on the public record under your verified name.
  2. The Facilitator reads everything.It merges duplicates into items, shows where people agree and where they split, drafts plain-language text, and checks every draft against the Rights Charter.
  3. You ratify.Every voter's weight is one. The result, the reasoning and the dissent are published together.
  4. Law.Enacted, funded from the Commons, audited in the open. Replayable by any citizen.
§3 · The Facilitator

A clerk, not a king.

The AI in Open Floor does the job Congress was supposed to do and never could at scale: listen to everyone, find the overlap, write it down clearly, and keep the books. It does not get a vote. It does not get an opinion.

This was impossible in 1789 and still impossible in 2015. Reading, merging and summarizing 150 million filings a day is now a solved problem. The decision layer is the only part that has not caught up.

What it does

  • Reads every filing, every day
  • Merges duplicates into items people can vote on
  • Shows where the country agrees and where it splits, by region, by age, by income
  • Drafts in plain language a 14-year-old can read
  • Checks every draft against the Rights Charter before it reaches a ballot
  • Runs ratification and publishes the count
  • Allocates the Commons to ratified priorities, line by line, in public
  • Logs its own reasoning so any decision can be replayed

What it never does

  • Vote
  • Hold a position on any item
  • Take money, from anyone, for anything
  • Run for anything
  • Override a ratified result
  • Draft against the Rights Charter
  • Act without an audit trail
  • Run as a single model from a single vendor

    §5 · The Commons

    Money follows the vote, not the other way around.

    Today money flows toward the people who decide, which is why five billion dollars a year goes into lobbying 535 of them. On the Floor there is nobody to lobby. Public money goes into one Commons and comes out by ratified priority, with an equal floor under every person first.

    In

    • Taxes, with rates set on the Floor
    • Returns on public stakes in land, minerals, spectrum, data and infrastructure
    • A public wealth fund that owns a slice of everything, the way Norway's does

    The Commons

    • One ledger, public to the cent
    • The Facilitator allocates to priorities voters ranked, in the order they ranked them
    • No earmarks, because there is nobody to earmark for
    • Every line item replayable: who asked, what was ranked, what was paid

    Out

    • The floor: an equal baseline for every person. Housing, health, food, education. Paid first, never ranked.
    • Priorities: everything else, funded in ranked order until the money runs out
    • The dividend: whatever is left, paid to every person equally
    The plumbing already exists.

    Alaska has paid every resident an equal dividend from oil since 1982. Norway's public fund owns about 1.5% of every listed company on earth. What is missing is not the money. It is the decision layer.

    §6 · The guardrails

    The questions that kill this idea if you do not answer them.

    Every reader will reach for one of these within thirty seconds. The concept lives or dies on the answers.

    Who controls the AI?

    Nobody, and everybody. Open weights, open training data, open logs. At least three independently built models must agree before anything reaches a ballot. Voters elect an audit board that can halt the whole system with one vote. Any citizen can replay any decision and get the same answer.

    What stops 51% from crushing 49%?

    A Rights Charter the Facilitator cannot draft against, and voters cannot amend without a two-thirds supermajority in two separate years. Majority rule for policy. Supermajority for rights. Same as a constitution, minus the people in the middle.

    Bots, trolls, foreign money?

    One verified person, one account, one weight. Filings are public and attributable. There is no official to bribe and no ad buy that changes a count. Influence has to persuade actual people, in the open, one at a time.

    Nobody has time to vote on everything.

    You don't. Follow the topics you care about. For the rest, lend your weight to a person or a stance you trust, and take it back any time. Your weight is always yours to recall.

    People vote on emotion too.

    Yes. 150 million emotions average out. One does not. The Facilitator also shows you what a draft costs and who it affects before you vote, which is more than any member of Congress gets today.

    What happens to Congress?

    It keeps its vote at first and loses its excuses. Then it becomes the audit board. See the roadmap.

    §7 · Precedents

    None of this is science fiction.

    Every piece of Open Floor is running somewhere today. Nobody has connected them.

    • 1982Alaska

      Permanent Fund Dividend. Every resident gets an equal share of oil revenue, every year, no questions asked.

    • 1989Porto Alegre, Brazil

      Participatory budgeting. Residents set part of the city budget directly. Now used in thousands of cities worldwide.

    • 2005Estonia

      Internet voting in national elections. About half of all votes are now cast online, with a verified digital identity.

    • 2015Taiwan

      vTaiwan. An AI-assisted consensus tool surfaced ride-share rules thousands of people could agree on, which became law.

    • 2016Barcelona

      Decidim. Open-source platform where residents propose, debate and vote on city plans. Adopted by hundreds of institutions since.

    • OngoingSwitzerland

      Binding national referendums several times a year on tax, immigration, pensions and more. The country has not fallen apart.

    §8 · Roadmap

    How you get there without a revolution.

    Nobody hands over the country to a new system in one step. Each phase is useful on its own and makes the next one obvious.

    Phase 1 · years 1–2

    The mirror

    Open Floor runs as an advisory layer. It reads what each district files and publishes, next to every roll-call vote, what the district actually wanted. Representatives keep their vote. They lose the excuse.

    Phase 2 · years 2–5

    The city

    Cities adopt binding Floor votes for their budgets. Participatory budgeting with a Facilitator instead of a town hall nobody attends.

    Phase 3 · years 5–10

    The state

    State ballot measures move onto the Floor. Signature drives are replaced by filings. The Rights Charter is tested in court and survives.

    Phase 4 · year 10+

    The floor

    Federal law is ratified on the Floor. The Commons replaces appropriations. Congress becomes the audit board that can halt the system, and nothing else.
